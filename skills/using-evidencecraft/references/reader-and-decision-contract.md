# Reader and Decision Contract

Read when framing a user decision, defining report delivery, synthesizing the reader artifact, or reviewing its usability. This is one shared presentation contract, not another review stage. Apply the resolved language contract. Scale the document to the decision; headings below are guidance, not mandatory output sections.

## Questions and approval

Lead with the decision the user needs to make and why it affects the result. Offer a recommendation and the consequence of each real alternative. Ask only unresolved material questions; combine tightly related choices when that reduces round trips. Keep paths, hashes, internal statuses, and implementation choices in linked audit records.

Existing explicit instructions and applicable standing decisions remain authorization. A Brief or Plan may record them without asking again. Record what was authorized and its actual source; do not claim the user approved a file they never saw. If the user requested a review/approval checkpoint, honor it. Unresolved scope, commitments, material metric meaning, or acceptable risk requires the relevant authority; silence is not approval.

Example: “建议按可用库存决定补货，在途采购单独列出，避免重复计算。把在途也算作可用，会减少本期建议补货量。你们的补货决策采用哪一种口径？” Link the definition for inspection; do not ask the user to approve a hash table.

## Plans and intermediate updates

Put the analysis proposal before execution detail: task understanding, data readiness and its business impact, recommended strategy with its basis, a report blueprint, and important limits or choices. The blueprint names questions, required evidence and intended presentation; it does not pretend that findings already exist. Keep executable interfaces available by link. Small work may use a few paragraphs and one Package; do not fill empty template sections or create artificial alternatives.

When critical readiness is unknown, explain what a limited Draft can cover, what remains unverified, and the recommended adjustment. Do not present it as executable or ask the user to confirm a technical checklist. Successful preparation adds no routine approval stop.

Progress explains what was learned, its implication, and the next useful action. Show a technical blocker only with its business consequence. A complete governance record does not require repeating it in chat, every handoff, or every report.

## Reader report

Start with the answer or recommended action and the evidence strength that matters to using it. For a decision report, the opening should let the intended reader identify what to act on first, why, the expected scale, and when the advice should not be followed. For descriptive or research reports, lead with the requested finding and its limits instead of inventing actions.

Organize remaining content around reader questions. Explain unfamiliar business measures at first use. Put a qualification beside the claim it changes, including assumptions behind dates, rates, totals, or rankings. Separate observation, inference, scenario, and recommendation.

Use a compact table for a real comparison, a figure when it explains a pattern more clearly, and prose for interpretation. Split tables containing different decisions; summarize long entity lists to the relevant priorities. A table the audience must scroll in two directions, decode through many abbreviations, or read at tiny type is a signal to redesign, not a formatting pass.

Keep key totals, priority items, reasoning, and material limitations in the report. Row-level calculations and exhaustive evidence may stay in governed files; do not copy them just to make the report “self-contained.” If the user requires a complete reader-facing detail file, declare and review it as a companion deliverable. The core conclusion must remain understandable without that file or repository access.

Use precision suited to the decision and consistent units. Preserve distinctions that change interpretation: display 0.324 days as 0.3 days, “less than 1 day,” or another justified expression, never an unexplained zero. Explain differing populations and overlapping quantities before a reader could add incompatible totals.

Internal Run/WP/Evidence IDs, hashes, review verdicts, and workflow logs belong in governance. Formal citations and business-relevant source explanations remain in the report.

## Change and review

The producer may shorten prose, split a table, improve precision, or reorganize a draft within the authorized meaning and delivery contract. These changes need review of the new bytes, not renewed business approval. A changed metric, population, claim meaning, or explicit delivery commitment follows its semantic owner.

The independent reviewer first reads the report as its intended audience, then checks evidence for consequential claims. Missing decision-critical information, misleading rounding, or inaccessible required content can block approval. A preferred wording or layout is an optional improvement unless it violates an explicit requirement or materially changes use. Do not add a separate readability reviewer or automatic rewrite loop.
